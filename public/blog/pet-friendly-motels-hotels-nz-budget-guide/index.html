Pet-Friendly Motels and Hotels in New Zealand: Budget Guide for Travellers with Pets

Published on 5 August 2026 | By ZiggySitters

Travelling with pets in New Zealand should not mean emptying your wallet on overpriced accommodation. From Kaikohe to Invercargill, there are hundreds of motels, motor lodges, and hotels that welcome four-legged guests without charging a fortune. This guide covers the best budget pet-friendly options across every major region, what to expect when you book, and how to keep costs down while keeping your pet comfortable on the road.

What to Expect from Pet-Friendly Motels in New Zealand

Most pet-friendly motels in New Zealand operate under straightforward rules. You can generally expect ground-floor units with direct outdoor access, which makes toilet breaks and walks far easier than upper-floor hotel rooms. Many motels provide a small grassy area or are located near parks and reserves where your dog can stretch its legs after a long drive.

Standard pet fees in New Zealand motels range from $10 to $30 per night per pet, though a surprising number of places do not charge extra at all. The key is to ask at the time of booking rather than showing up unannounced with a golden retriever. Some motels require pets to be crated when left unattended in the room, while others are happy for well-behaved animals to relax on the bed.

New Zealand motels tend to be more pet-tolerant than hotels, largely because they are owner-operated and the units have separate entrances. This means no carrying a squirming cat through a lobby full of strangers. Motels are also more likely to have laundries on-site, which is handy after a muddy walk along the West Coast or a day at the beach.

Auckland and Northland

Auckland has a decent spread of pet-friendly motels, particularly in the southern and western suburbs where rates are lower. In Manukau and Papakura, you will find motor lodges charging $120 to $160 per night for a unit that welcomes one or two small to medium dogs. North Shore motels are slightly pricier but many accept pets if you call ahead, with units close to the numerous off-lead beaches along the coastline.

Heading north, the Bay of Islands and Whangarei areas have a strong selection of pet-friendly holiday parks and motels. Many coastal properties in Paihia and Russell allow dogs on the grounds, and some even have enclosed yards. Budget around $100 to $140 per night in the off-season. Northland holiday parks with cabin accommodation often charge just $5 to $15 per pet per night, making them one of the cheapest options for travelling families with dogs.

For cats, motels are generally more restrictive. Many that welcome dogs will accept cats in carriers, but few advertise this openly. It is always worth a phone call. The Kaipara region has a handful of farm stays that welcome both cats and dogs, often at rates below $130 per night with pet fees included.

Waikato and Bay of Plenty

Hamilton and the surrounding Waikato region offer some of the best-value pet-friendly motels in the country. Units in Hamilton Central and Chartwell start from around $110 per night, and many have courtyard or garden access. The region's flat terrain and extensive walkways along the Waikato River make it easy to exercise your dog without needing a car.

Tauranga and Mount Maunganui have a growing number of pet-friendly options, particularly in the older motor lodges along Cameron Road and the southern outskirts. Rates range from $130 to $180 in peak season, dropping to $100 to $140 outside school holidays. Several motels near the Tauranga harbour allow small dogs on the balconies and have grass strips out the front for quick toilet stops.

Whakatane and Rotorua are worth considering if you are touring the geothermal region. Rotorua motels tend to be spacious and many are pet-friendly, with rates around $120 to $160 per night. The Redwoods and other forest walks are dog-friendly outside of conservation zones, making Rotorua an excellent base for a budget pet-friendly holiday.

Central North Island and Hawke's Bay

Taupo and Turangi motels frequently accept pets, particularly the larger motor lodges with ground-floor units and grass areas. Taupo's lakefront motels can be pricey in summer, but those on the main highway a few blocks back often offer pet-friendly rooms from $110 to $140 per night. The lakefront walkways are open to dogs on leads, providing easy exercise without leaving town.

Napier and Hastings in Hawke's Bay are increasingly pet-aware. Several Art Deco motels in central Napier allow small dogs, though you should confirm the pet policy directly as it changes seasonally. Hastings has cheaper options, with motor lodges around $100 to $130 per night and easy access to dog-friendly beaches at Clifton and Haumoana. The region's wineries are also becoming more pet-friendly in their outdoor areas, which is a bonus for travelling couples.

Wellington and the Lower North Island

Wellington presents a challenge for pet-friendly budget accommodation, partly because of its hilly terrain and limited flat motel strips. The Lower Hutt and Petone areas are your best bet, with several motor lodges charging $120 to $160 per night and accepting small to medium dogs. Petone's beachfront is dog-friendly, so morning and evening walks are sorted.

Further north in the Manawatu, Palmerston North motels are among the cheapest pet-friendly options in the North Island. Units from $95 to $130 per night are common, and the Esplanade and Hokowhitu Park provide excellent off-lead exercise areas. The Manawatu Gorge walk is a popular stop if you are passing through with an active dog.

Wanganui motels are similarly affordable and pet-friendly. The riverfront area has several motels that welcome pets at rates around $90 to $120 per night, and the extensive riverside walkways make it easy to tire out even the most energetic kelpie before bedtime.

Canterbury and the South Island East Coast

Christchurch has rebuilt much of its motel stock since the earthquakes, and pet-friendly options are now plentiful. Riccarton and Addington motor lodges offer pet-friendly units from $110 to $150 per night, many with easy access to Hagley Park's extensive off-lead areas. The Avon River precinct is also walkable with dogs on leads, making central Christchurch more pet-friendly than many visitors expect.

Heading south through Timaru and Oamaru, motel rates drop further. Timaru motels often charge $90 to $120 per night and several accept pets without an additional fee. Oamaru's Victorian precinct and harbour area are charming for a dog walk, and the nearby beaches are mostly off-lead outside of nesting season.

Further south, Dunedin motels in the central city and South Dunedin areas typically range from $100 to $140 per night. The Otago Peninsula and numerous town belt walkways give dogs plenty of exercise options. Invercargill motels are among the cheapest in the country, with pet-friendly units from $85 to $110 per night, making Southland an affordable base for exploring the Catlins and Stewart Island surrounds.

West Coast and Alpine Regions

The West Coast is a paradise for travelling dogs. Greymouth, Hokitika, and Westport motels are affordable and overwhelmingly pet-friendly, with rates from $90 to $130 per night. Many have large grass areas and are close to dog-friendly beaches and bush walks. The West Coast's relaxed attitude to pets means you will rarely be turned away if you ask politely.

In the alpine regions, Queenstown and Wanaka are pricier but still have budget pet options if you book early. Motels on the outskirts of Queenstown, particularly around Frankton and Kelvin Heights, offer pet-friendly rooms from $150 to $200 per night in peak season. Wanaka has fewer options but those that exist are excellent, with lakeside access and mountain walks right from the motel door.

For ski season travellers, note that most ski field carparks do not allow dogs left in vehicles. If you are planning a ski trip, arrange a local pet sitter through ZiggySitters for the day rather than leaving your dog in the motel room for extended periods.

Tips for Keeping Costs Down

  • Book directly with the motel rather than through third-party platforms. Many motels offer a direct-booking discount of 5 to 10 percent, and you can negotiate the pet fee more easily.
  • Travel outside school holidays and long weekends. New Zealand motel rates can double during peak periods, and pet-friendly rooms are the first to sell out.
  • Consider holiday park cabins. Many holiday parks across NZ offer basic cabin accommodation from $70 to $100 per night and are far more likely to accept pets than hotels. Facilities are shared, but for a one or two night stopover, they are hard to beat on price.
  • Travel with a crate. Motels that require pets to be crated when unattended are more likely to accept larger dogs, and having a crate removes any concern about damage.
  • Ask about weekly rates. If you are staying in one place for five or more nights, many motels will discount by 10 to 20 percent and may waive the pet fee entirely.
  • Pack your pet's own bedding and bowls. Some motels charge a cleaning surcharge if they need to wash pet hair off furniture. Bringing your own blanket and food dishes avoids this entirely.

What to Ask Before You Book

Not all pet-friendly motels are created equal. Before confirming a booking, ask these questions:

  • Is there a pet fee, and if so, how much per night per pet?
  • Are there weight or breed restrictions?
  • Is the unit ground floor with direct outdoor access?
  • Are pets allowed to be left unattended in the room? If so, must they be crated?
  • Is there a grass area nearby for toilet breaks?
  • Are cats accepted, or only dogs?
  • Is there a refundable damage deposit?
  • Are there nearby off-lead exercise areas?

Getting clear answers upfront avoids surprises at check-in and ensures your pet has a comfortable stay. The best pet-friendly motels will answer all of these questions without hesitation.

Making the Most of Your Pet-Friendly NZ Road Trip

New Zealand is one of the best countries in the world for road-tripping with pets. Motels and holiday parks are widely distributed, distances between towns are manageable, and the landscape offers endless walking opportunities. By choosing budget pet-friendly accommodation, asking the right questions before you book, and travelling outside peak periods, you can explore Aotearoa with your pet without breaking the bank.

Whether you are heading to the beaches of Northland, the vineyards of Hawke's Bay, or the glaciers of the West Coast, there is a pet-friendly room waiting for you at a price that will not ruin the holiday budget. Plan ahead, pack your pet's essentials, and enjoy the journey.
