Pet-Friendly Ferry & Boat Travel in New Zealand

Published 5 August 2026 · 9 min read

New Zealand is an island nation, and travelling between the North and South Islands — or heading out to the Hauraki Gulf, Marlborough Sounds, or Bay of Islands — often means getting on a boat. If you have a dog or cat, that raises practical questions: can they come on board? Where do they go? Is it safe? This guide covers everything Kiwi pet owners need to know about ferry and boat travel with pets, from Cook Strait crossings to harbour commuter runs.

Inter-Island Ferry Travel: Cook Strait Crossings

The Cook Strait crossing between Wellington and Picton is the most common long-distance ferry route for New Zealand pet owners. Two operators serve this route — Interislander and Bluebridge — and both allow pets, but with different policies.

Interislander Pet Policy

Interislander allows dogs and small pets on all sailings. Dogs travel in a dedicated pet area on the car deck in a secure, ventilated enclosure. You must check your dog in at the vehicle check-in area — they cannot walk through the passenger lounges. Cats and other small animals must remain in their carriers for the entire crossing. The pet area is covered and protected from weather, but it is not climate-controlled, so consider the season when booking. There is no additional charge for pets on Interislander.

Bluebridge Pet Policy

Bluebridge also permits pets on Cook Strait sailings. Dogs are accommodated in a pet area on the car deck, similar to Interislander, while cats and small animals must stay in approved carriers. Bluebridge staff are generally helpful during boarding, but you should arrive early to allow time for pet check-in. Like Interislander, there is no extra fee for travelling with a pet.

Tips for the Cook Strait Crossing

  • Book early during peak season — summer and school holiday sailings fill quickly, and pet spaces are limited on each vessel
  • Walk your dog before boarding — there are green areas near both Wellington and Picton terminals for a last toilet break
  • Pack a familiar blanket or toy — the car deck pet area is unfamiliar, and scent can reduce anxiety
  • Avoid feeding right before departure — motion sickness affects dogs too, and a rough Cook Strait crossing can trigger vomiting
  • Check weather forecasts — rough crossings are common. If your pet is prone to motion sickness, consult your vet about anti-nausea medication before the trip
  • Never leave a pet in your car on the car deck — this is strictly prohibited and potentially fatal in enclosed, hot vehicle spaces

Auckland Harbour Ferries: Waiheke, Devonport & Beyond

Auckland's commuter ferries are operated by Fullers360 and Explore, and their pet policies are relatively pet-friendly compared to many international cities.

Waiheke Island Ferries

Fullers360 allows leashed dogs on Waiheke Island passenger ferries during off-peak times (outside weekday commuter hours of 7–9 AM and 4:30–6:30 PM). Dogs must be on a short lead, muzzled if required, and well-behaved around other passengers. Cats and small animals must remain in enclosed carriers at all times. Waiheke Island itself is a fantastic destination for dogs — numerous beaches allow off-leash exercise, and many vineyards are dog-friendly.

Devonport & Inner Harbour Ferries

The short Devonport and inner harbour ferry routes permit small dogs in carriers. Leashed dogs are generally not permitted on these shorter commuter runs during peak hours, but policies can vary by vessel. Always check the Fullers360 website for the latest pet policy before travelling.

Harbour Ferry Tips

  • Travel off-peak when possible — ferries are less crowded and crew are more accommodating
  • Keep your dog calm and close — Auckland ferries can be busy, and sudden noises may startle anxious animals
  • Bring waste bags — you are responsible for any accidents on board
  • Some Waiheke holiday homes are explicitly pet-friendly — search for "pet-friendly" on accommodation platforms

Regional Ferry & Boat Services

Marlborough Sounds

The Marlborough Sounds are serviced by several private water taxi and charter operators departing from Picton and Havelock. Most private charters welcome well-behaved dogs on board, especially those operated by locals who understand the lifestyle. Beachcomber Fun Cruises and other Sounds operators generally accept leashed dogs — confirm when booking.

Bay of Islands & Northland

Ferries to Russell and charter boats in the Bay of Islands usually allow dogs at the skipper's discretion. The car ferry between Opua and Oki Point (Russell route) accommodates dogs in vehicles. For day cruises and island-hopping tours, always call ahead — many skippers are happy to have a calm dog on board.

Stewart Island

The Stewart Island ferry (Southern Express) departs from Bluff and takes about an hour. Dogs are generally not permitted due to Stewart Island's conservation status and kiwi population. If you must travel to Stewart Island with a pet, you will need to arrange private charter transport and check DOC requirements for pet access. Cats are strictly prohibited.

Great Barrier Island (Aotea)

The Sealink ferry to Great Barrier Island permits dogs in vehicles on the car deck, similar to Cook Strait ferries. Foot passengers with dogs must check with the operator in advance. Great Barrier is a superb dog-friendly destination with extensive beaches and walking tracks.

Private Boat & Charter Travel with Pets

If you own a boat or are hiring one for the day, bringing your pet along is usually straightforward, but safety is paramount.

Essential Safety Equipment

  • Pet life jacket — mandatory for any open-water travel. Measure your dog's chest and weight for correct sizing. Cats rarely tolerate life jackets, so keep them in secure carriers
  • Non-slip matting — boat decks are slippery for paws. Rubber matting gives your dog traction and confidence
  • Shade and fresh water — UV reflection off water intensifies sun exposure. Bring extra drinking water
  • Secure carrier or harness — sudden boat movement can send an unsecured pet overboard. Always tether or crate
  • Poo bags and paper towels — accidents happen, especially on first boat trips

Introducing Your Pet to the Boat

Before heading out on the water, let your dog explore the boat at the marina or on the trailer. Turn the engine on without leaving the dock. Reward calm behaviour with treats. Most dogs adjust to boat travel within two to three short trips, but some need more time. If your dog shows extreme anxiety, consider leaving them with a trusted pet sitter instead.

Water Safety for Pets

New Zealand's coastal waters demand respect. Even strong swimming dogs can get into trouble in tidal currents, especially around harbour mouths and rocky coastlines.

  • Not all dogs swim naturally — bulldogs, pugs, and other brachycephalic breeds often cannot swim. Never assume your dog can swim
  • Rip currents and tides — be especially cautious at west coast beaches (Muriwai, Piha, Raglan) where rips are common
  • Bluebottles and jellyfish — common on NZ beaches in summer. If your dog licks or eats a washed-up jellyfish, contact your vet immediately
  • Boat ramps — slippery and often covered in marine growth. Keep your dog on lead around boat ramps
  • Salt water ingestion — dogs drinking sea water can suffer salt poisoning. Always carry fresh water on boat trips

Pet-Friendly Islands & Destinations

Some of New Zealand's best island destinations welcome pets, while others are strict conservation zones. Here is a quick reference:

  • Waiheke Island — Very pet-friendly. Many beaches allow off-leash dogs, and numerous holiday homes welcome pets
  • Great Barrier Island — Dog-friendly with extensive beaches. Leashed dogs on tracks, off-leash at designated beaches
  • Stewart Island — Dogs generally not permitted due to kiwi and wildlife. Cats strictly prohibited
  • Kapiti Island — No pets allowed. Strict nature reserve
  • Marlborough Sounds — Generally pet-friendly, especially on private charters and baches. Keep dogs leashed near wildlife areas
  • Bay of Islands — Mixed. Town beaches often allow leashed dogs. Check local council bylaws for each beach

Pet Motion Sickness: Prevention & Treatment

Just like humans, many pets experience motion sickness on boats. Signs include excessive drooling, lip licking, restlessness, and vomiting. Prevention strategies include:

  • Fasting before travel — withhold food for 4–6 hours before departure
  • Fresh air — position your pet where they can see the horizon if possible, or keep them in a covered carrier to reduce visual stimulus
  • Veterinary medication — talk to your vet about Cerenia (maropitant) or other anti-nausea medications specifically for motion sickness in dogs
  • Ginger treats — some pet owners report ginger helps with mild nausea (check with your vet first)
  • Desensitisation — short practice trips before a long ferry crossing can help your pet adjust

When to Leave Your Pet Behind

Sometimes the kindest option is to leave your pet with a trusted sitter. Consider leaving your pet at home if:

  • The ferry crossing forecast is rough and your pet is anxious or prone to motion sickness
  • You are visiting a conservation area where pets are prohibited
  • Your pet is elderly, injured, or recovering from surgery
  • Your dog is reactive or anxious around strangers in confined spaces
  • You are travelling to Stewart Island or any DOC-managed island
