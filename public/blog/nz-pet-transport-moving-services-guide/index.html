NZ Pet Transport & Moving Services: Flying, Driving & Relocating Pets Across New Zealand

Whether you're relocating from Auckland to Wellington, moving from the North Island to the South Island, or simply need to get your pet from one end of New Zealand to another, understanding your pet transport options is essential. New Zealand's unique geography — two main islands connected by ferry, remote rural areas, and limited domestic flight routes — means pet relocation requires more planning than in most countries. This guide covers every option available to Kiwi pet owners in 2026, with costs, timelines, and practical advice for each method.

Why Pet Transport in New Zealand Is Different

New Zealand presents specific challenges for pet transport that don't exist in larger, more connected countries. The Cook Strait crossing between the North and South Islands requires careful planning, as the Interislander and Bluebridge ferries have different pet policies. Domestic airlines have strict pet cargo requirements and limited routes. Many rural areas — from the Far North to the West Coast — lack direct transport services, requiring multi-leg journeys. And New Zealand's biosecurity rules, while primarily focused on international arrivals, also affect some domestic movements, particularly for birds and reptiles.

The good news is that New Zealand's pet transport industry has matured significantly. Professional pet transport companies now operate nationwide, offering door-to-door services that handle everything from vet checks to airline bookings. Pet-friendly accommodation options have expanded along major routes, and the growth of platforms like ZiggySitters means you can often find a trusted sitter willing to transport your pet as part of a house-sitting arrangement.

Flying with Pets: Domestic Air Travel

Air New Zealand is the primary carrier for domestic pet transport in New Zealand. Pets travel in the cargo hold — there is no cabin option for companion animals on domestic NZ flights (only certified service dogs may travel in the cabin). This means your pet will be in an IATA-approved travel crate in a pressurised, temperature-controlled cargo compartment.

Costs for Domestic Pet Flights (2026)

  • Small pet (under 10kg with crate): $75–$120 per flight segment
  • Medium pet (10–25kg with crate): $120–$200 per flight segment
  • Large pet (25–40kg with crate): $200–$350 per flight segment
  • Extra-large breeds (over 40kg): $350–$500+ per flight segment
  • IATA-approved crate (if you need to buy one): $80–$350 depending on size

These prices are for the pet cargo fee only — you'll also pay your own airfare. Most domestic routes are available, including Auckland to Wellington, Auckland to Christchurch, Auckland to Queenstown, Wellington to Christchurch, and Auckland to Dunedin. Smaller regional airports like Napier, Palmerston North, and Invercargill also accept pet cargo, but flights are less frequent and may require connections through major hubs.

Booking Tips for Domestic Pet Flights

Always book your pet's cargo space at the same time as your own ticket — there are limited spots per flight and they fill up, especially during school holidays and Christmas. Call Air New Zealand directly rather than booking online, as the pet cargo system requires manual coordination. Your vet will need to confirm your pet is fit to fly, and you should avoid sedation (which can cause respiratory issues at altitude). For brachycephalic breeds like French Bulldogs, Pugs, and British Shorthair cats, discuss air travel risks with your vet — these breeds are more susceptible to breathing difficulties during flights.

Road Transport: Driving with Your Pet

Driving is often the most practical and least stressful option for domestic pet transport within New Zealand, particularly for short to medium distances. It gives you full control over stops, temperature, feeding times, and your pet's comfort. For North Island journeys, driving is frequently faster than flying once you factor in airport arrival times, cargo check-in, and collection at the destination.

Essential Road Trip Preparations

Before a long drive, ensure your pet is comfortable in the car. Start with short trips to build tolerance, especially if your pet associates car travel with vet visits. Use a properly fitted harness or travel crate — never let pets roam loose in the vehicle. New Zealand law requires animals to be secured, and a loose pet can become a dangerous projectile in an accident.

Pack a pet travel kit with water, a portable bowl, food, medications, leads, waste bags, a familiar blanket or toy, and your vet's contact details. Plan your route around rest stops every 2–3 hours for dogs and ensure cats remain in their carrier throughout the journey. Many NZ rest areas along State Highways now have designated pet exercise areas — particularly on SH1, SH2, and the main routes through the Central North Island.

Pet-Friendly Accommodation on Long Drives

For journeys longer than 4–5 hours, an overnight stop may be necessary. New Zealand has a growing number of pet-friendly motels, holiday parks, and B&Bs. Booking platforms like Bookabach and some Bachcare listings allow pet-specific searches. Many Holiday Parks (formerly TOP 10) accept pets in designated cabins and sites. Expect to pay a pet surcharge of $10–$30 per night at most establishments. Always confirm pet policies directly with the property before booking — online listings aren't always up to date.

Inter-Island Travel: Ferry Crossing with Pets

The Cook Strait ferry is the key link for any pet moving between the North and South Islands. Both major operators — Interislander (Kiwirail) and Bluebridge (StraitNZ) — accept pets, but with different procedures and costs.

Interislander Pet Policy

Pets travel in a purpose-built, ventilated vehicle deck area. You cannot access your pet during the crossing. Check-in is 45 minutes before departure for vehicles with pets. The pet fee is approximately $30–$45 per animal per crossing. You must book pet transport in advance — it cannot be added at the terminal. Dogs must be on leads during check-in and disembarkation. Cats and small animals must remain in secure carriers at all times.

Bluebridge Pet Policy

Bluebridge also carries pets in a dedicated vehicle deck area, with similar pricing ($25–$40 per animal). Their ferries have a slightly more relaxed check-in process, but the same fundamental rules apply: pets stay in the vehicle area during the crossing, no access during the sail, and advance booking is required.

Tips for Ferry Crossings

Book pet transport when you book your ferry ticket — pet spots are limited. Arrive early and walk your dog before check-in, as they'll be confined for 3–4 hours. For cats, a familiar-smelling blanket in the carrier can reduce stress. If your pet is particularly anxious, consider the night sailing when the ferry is typically quieter. In rough weather, the Cook Strait can be challenging — if your pet is prone to motion sickness, consult your vet about anti-nausea medication before the trip.

Professional Pet Transport Services

If you can't transport your pet yourself, several professional services operate across New Zealand. These companies handle door-to-door transport, including vet checks, crate provision, airline bookings, and delivery to your new address.

Leading NZ Pet Transport Companies (2026)

  • Pet Express NZ — Nationwide door-to-door service, specialises in long-distance and inter-island relocations. Costs from $350–$800 depending on distance and pet size.
  • Jetpets NZ — Premium service with real-time tracking, temperature-controlled vehicles, and vet partnerships. From $400–$1,200 for full-service relocations.
  • Pet Movers New Zealand — Budget-friendly option with shared transport routes running weekly between major centres. From $200–$500.
  • Animal Airline Services — Specialises in air cargo coordination for domestic and international moves. From $300–$600 for domestic.

Professional transport is significantly more expensive than DIY but removes all the logistical complexity. Most services include regular photo updates, GPS tracking, and a dedicated coordinator. Booking 2–4 weeks in advance is recommended, especially during peak moving periods (January–March and school holidays).

Pet Transport for Small Animals, Birds & Reptiles

Cats and dogs dominate pet transport discussions, but New Zealand has specific rules for other animals. Rabbits, guinea pigs, and ferrets can travel by road or air but need secure, ventilated carriers. Birds are subject to stricter regulations — some native species cannot be transported without DOC permits, and even domestic bird transport between regions may require health documentation. Reptiles need temperature-controlled environments and are best transported by specialist services rather than standard pet transport companies. Fish are the most challenging — they require specialised containers, water treatment, and are generally best rehomed locally rather than transported over long distances.

Pet Safety During Transport

Regardless of transport method, prioritise your pet's safety and comfort. Ensure vaccinations are current and obtain a health certificate from your vet within 10 days of travel. Microchip your pet and update the NZ Companion Animal Register with your new address before moving. Pack a pet first-aid kit including bandages, antiseptic wipes, tweezers, and any regular medications. Never leave pets unattended in vehicles, especially during summer — NZ temperatures can turn dangerous in minutes.

For anxious pets, consider a trial run before the actual move. A short car trip or overnight stay at a friend's house can reveal potential issues and help your pet build confidence. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I take my pet on public transport in New Zealand?

Only certified assistance dogs are allowed on most public transport. Auckland Transport, Metlink Wellington, and Environment Canterbury all prohibit pets on buses and trains, except for service animals. Some ferry services make exceptions for small pets in carriers, but you must confirm directly with the operator.

Do I need documentation to move my pet between the North and South Islands?

No domestic biosecurity documentation is required for dogs and cats moving between the islands. However, birds, reptiles, and some exotic species may require permits or health certificates. Always check with the Ministry for Primary Industries (MPI) before transporting non-standard pets.

How far in advance should I book pet transport?

For domestic flights, book 2–4 weeks ahead, longer during holidays. Professional transport services need 1–2 weeks minimum. Ferry pet spots should be booked when you book your ferry ticket. DIY driving requires no advance booking but plan your route and accommodation.
