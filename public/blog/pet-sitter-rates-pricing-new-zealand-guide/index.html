Pet Sitter Rates and Pricing in New Zealand: The Complete 2026 Guide

One of the first questions Kiwi pet owners ask when looking for a pet sitter is: how much should it cost? The answer depends on the type of service, where you live, how many pets you have, and what level of care you need. This guide breaks down pet sitting rates across New Zealand so you can budget accurately and understand what you are paying for.

What Affects Pet Sitting Rates

Number of Pets

Most sitters include the first pet in their base rate and charge an additional fee for extra animals:

  • Second dog: $10–$20 per night
  • Third dog or more: $5–$15 per night each
  • Cats (additional): $5–$10 per night each
  • Small animals (rabbits, guinea pigs, birds): $5–$10 per night each
  • Some sitters include a second pet free, especially cats or small animals that do not require walks

Pet Type and Breed

Not all pets are equally easy to care for. Sitters often adjust rates based on:

  • Puppies: Higher rates due to frequent feeding, toilet training, and supervision needs. Expect $10–$20 above the standard rate.
  • Senior dogs: May cost more if they need medication, help with mobility, or more frequent toilet breaks.
  • Large or high-energy breeds: Some sitters charge more for dogs over 25kg or breeds that need extended exercise.
  • Reactive or anxious dogs: Sitters experienced with behavioural issues often charge a premium for specialist care.
  • Exotic pets: Reptiles, birds, and fish often require specialist knowledge and may command higher rates.

Duration and Frequency

Longer bookings are often discounted. Many sitters offer:

  • Weekly rates: 5–10% discount on stays of 7+ nights
  • Monthly rates: 10–15% discount on stays of 28+ nights
  • Regular clients: Loyalty discounts for recurring weekly visits

If you travel frequently for work, ask your sitter about a standing arrangement. Many will offer a reduced rate for guaranteed, regular bookings.

The single most expensive time to book a pet sitter in New Zealand is the Christmas and New Year period from 20 December to 10 January. Sitters are booked out months in advance and rates can be double the off-season price.

Additional Services

  • Medication administration: Usually included for simple pills. Injectable medication or complex regimens may add $5–$15 per day.
  • Plant watering and garden care: Often included in house sitting. May add $5–$10 per visit for drop-in services.
  • Mail and parcel collection: Generally included at no extra cost.
  • Extended walks: A standard 30-minute walk is usually included; a 60-minute walk or a second daily walk may add $10–$20.
  • Grooming or bathing: $15–$30 if the sitter offers it.
  • Pet taxi: $30–$60 per trip for vet visits, grooming, or transport.

How to Get the Best Value from Your Pet Sitter

Book Early

The single most effective way to get good rates is to book early. Sitters who are fully booked two weeks before Christmas can charge premium rates. Sitters who have gaps in their calendar in February may offer discounts. Plan ahead and you will have more choice and better prices.

Be Honest About Your Pet’s Needs

Under-describing your pet’s behaviour or medical needs to get a lower rate backfires. The sitter may struggle, your pet may not get the care they need, and you may face additional charges or a cancelled booking. Describe your pet accurately and you will find a sitter who is genuinely a good match.

Consider Mid-Week and Off-Peak Dates

If your travel dates are flexible, avoid Friday and Saturday departures during school holidays. Mid-week bookings are easier to fill and sitters may offer better rates for dates that plug gaps in their calendar.

Bundling Services

If you need a pet sitter regularly (for shift work, frequent business travel, or ongoing dog walking), ask about package deals. Many sitters offer reduced rates for:

  • Recurring weekly dog walks (4+ per week)
  • Regular overnight sits (1–2 nights per week for shift workers)
  • Extended stays (7+ nights)
  • Multi-pet households

Communicate Clearly

The best sitter-client relationships are built on clear expectations. Provide a detailed care sheet covering feeding times, portions, medication, walk routes, behaviour quirks, and emergency contacts. This reduces back-and-forth during the sit and ensures your pet gets consistent care.

What Pet Sitters Are Paying For

When you hire a pet sitter, you are not just paying for someone to feed your cat. You are paying for:

  • Time and attention: Overnight stays mean the sitter is giving up their evening and sleeping at your home instead of theirs.
  • Responsibility: Your pet’s health, safety, and wellbeing are in their hands. That carries real liability and stress.
  • Expertise: Experienced sitters can recognise early signs of illness, manage medication, handle behavioural issues, and respond to emergencies.
  • Insurance and registration: Professional sitters carry public liability insurance and may be registered with NZ pet sitting associations.
  • Travel: Drop-in visits involve driving to and from your home, often multiple times per day. Fuel and time are real costs.
  • Opportunity cost: A sitter who takes a 10-day booking cannot accept other work during that period.

Red Flags: When a Rate Is Too Low

If a sitter’s rates are significantly below market average, ask why. Very low rates can indicate:

  • No insurance or professional membership
  • Limited experience with pets
  • No background checks or verification
  • They may cancel if a better-paying booking comes along
  • They may not provide the level of care your pet needs

This does not mean every affordable sitter is a risk. Students, retirees, and animal lovers new to the profession may charge less while still providing excellent care. The key is verification: read reviews, check for insurance, and always do a meet-and-greet before booking.

Never pay in cash outside the platform. If a sitter asks you to pay directly to avoid platform fees, this is a red flag. Platform payments protect both parties with dispute resolution, insurance coverage, and a record of the transaction.

Tipping Pet Sitters in New Zealand

Tipping is not part of New Zealand culture, and that extends to pet sitters. You are not expected to tip. However, if your sitter went above and beyond, a tip or small gift is a lovely gesture:

  • A thank-you card with a $20–$50 voucher (Countdown, petrol, or a local cafe)
  • A five-star review on the platform — genuinely the most valuable thing you can give a sitter
  • A small gift related to their interests if you know them well
  • Rebooking them directly for your next trip

Pet Sitting for Shift Workers: A Special Case

If you work shifts (nursing, emergency services, hospitality, or trades), pet sitting costs work differently. Shift workers often need:

  • Early morning walks (5–7am) before a shift
  • Evening feeds and toilet breaks (7–10pm) after a late shift
  • Overnight stays when working night shifts
  • Unusual schedules that change week to week

For shift workers, a regular pet sitter on a standing arrangement is often cheaper than ad-hoc bookings. Many sitters offer discounted weekly rates for guaranteed, recurring work. Expect to pay $150–$250 per week for a dog that needs daily walks and one overnight stay, depending on your location.

The True Cost of Cheap Pet Care

It can be tempting to choose the cheapest option, especially for longer trips. But consider the hidden costs of cutting corners:

  • Vet bills from stress-related illness: A dog that develops diarrhoea or anxiety in a noisy kennel may need a $150–$300 vet visit on top of the boarding fee.
  • Property damage: An anxious pet left alone between drop-in visits may chew furniture, scratch doors, or have accidents that require professional cleaning.
  • Escaped pets: A sitter who does not close gates properly can lead to a lost pet, search costs, and council impound fees ($100–$300 plus daily boarding).
  • Peace of mind: A verified, reviewed, insured sitter on a platform gives you recourse if something goes wrong. A $15-per-visit sitter from a classifieds site gives you none of that.

The right sitter at a fair rate is an investment in your pet’s wellbeing and your own peace of mind while you are away.
