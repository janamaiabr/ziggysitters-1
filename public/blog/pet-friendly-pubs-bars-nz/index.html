Pet-Friendly Pubs, Bars & Breweries in New Zealand: Where to Drink with Your Dog

New Zealand's craft beer scene is booming, and the good news for dog owners is that many of our best pubs and breweries are just as enthusiastic about four-legged visitors. From Auckland's waterfront brewpubs to Wellington's wind-sheltered beer gardens and Christchurch's revitalised hospitality strip, here is your complete guide to enjoying a pint with your pup in Aotearoa.

The Law: Dogs in NZ Licensed Premises

Under the Sale and Supply of Alcohol Act 2012 and the Food Act 2014, dogs are not permitted in indoor areas where food is prepared or served. This includes the main bar floor of most pubs. However, dogs are generally welcome in outdoor dining areas, beer gardens, courtyards, and covered verandas provided the venue owner consents. The key distinction is between designated dining areas (no dogs indoors) and outdoor social areas where food is not the primary focus.

Assistance dogs certified under the Dog Control Act 1996 are an exception — they may accompany their handler anywhere the public can go, including indoor bar and restaurant areas. If you have an assistance dog, carry your certification card. Venue staff are entitled to ask for verification.

Individual venue policies vary widely. Some breweries embrace dogs wholeheartedly with water stations and dog menus, while others restrict dogs to specific areas or times. Always check the venue's website or social media before visiting, especially on busy nights or event weekends.

Auckland: Waterfront Breweries and Urban Beer Gardens

Auckland's craft beer scene is centred on the waterfront and inner suburbs, and many venues have expansive outdoor areas perfect for dogs. Behemoth Brewing in Kingsland has a large outdoor courtyard with a relaxed, dog-welcoming policy. Water bowls appear automatically and the staff know most local dogs by name. Their hazy IPAs are excellent, and the food menu is surprisingly good for a brewery.

Steam Brewing in Orakei sits right on the waterfront with outdoor tables overlooking the harbour. Dogs are welcome in the outdoor area and the sea breeze keeps everyone cool on summer afternoons. Deep Creek Brewing in Henderson has a spacious beer garden that is popular with west Auckland dog owners, especially after a walk in the Waitākere Ranges.

In the city centre, Fort Street Union has a covered outdoor area on the street that welcomes well-behaved dogs. Jefferson's on Galway Street in Newmarket has a dog-friendly courtyard and a solid craft beer selection. For a proper pub experience, The Kingslander in Kingsland has outdoor seating and a welcoming attitude towards dogs during daylight hours.

Wellington: The Craft Capital Goes Canine

Wellington claims more craft breweries per capita than anywhere else in New Zealand, and the city's compact walkable layout means you can easily hop between dog-friendly venues. Garage Project on Aro Street has outdoor seating and a dog-friendly atmosphere — the Aro Valley neighbourhood is one of Wellington's most pet-friendly areas. Whistling Sisters on Dixon Street has covered outdoor seating right in the heart of the city.

Hashigo Zake on Taranaki Street is Wellington's iconic craft beer bar with a small outdoor courtyard. While the indoor bar is off-limits, the courtyard is a social hub for craft beer lovers and their dogs. Bin44 on the waterfront has outdoor tables with harbour views and a welcoming policy for well-behaved dogs on leash.

For a true Wellington experience, walk the waterfront from Oriental Bay to Te Papa — dogs on leash are welcome along the promenade — and stop at Mac's Brewery on the waterfront. Their outdoor area is one of the best dog-friendly spots in the capital, with harbour views and solid pub food.

Christchurch: Rebuilt and Dog-Friendly

Post-quake Christchurch has reinvented its hospitality scene with an emphasis on outdoor spaces, making it more dog-friendly than ever. The Wood on Riccarton Road has a large outdoor area that regularly hosts dogs. Cassels & Sons in Woolston is a craft brewery with a massive outdoor space, food trucks, and a dog-welcoming culture that makes it one of the most popular weekend destinations in the city.

Three Boys Brewery in Heathcote has a spacious taproom with outdoor seating where dogs are welcome. The Brewery at The Tannery in Woolston is an upscale venue with dog-friendly outdoor courtyard seating. Pomeroy's Old Brewery Inn on Kilmore Street is one of Christchurch's oldest pubs and has a beer garden that welcomes dogs during the day.

Regional Highlights: Best Dog-Friendly Pubs Outside the Main Centres

Queenstown: Altitude Brewing by the lake has outdoor seating with mountain views and a dog-friendly policy. Smith's Brewery on Gorge Road also welcomes dogs in their outdoor area.

Hawke's Bay: Brave Brewing in Hastings has a large outdoor space that welcomes dogs. The region's wine trail also has several dog-friendly cellar doors, though policies vary — always check ahead.

Nelson: Founders Park Brewery has a beautiful garden setting where dogs are welcome. McCashin's Brewery in Stoke also has outdoor seating and a relaxed dog policy.

Dunedin: Emerson's Brewery in South Dunedin has a tasting area with outdoor space that welcomes dogs. Speight's Ale House on the waterfront has outdoor tables with harbour views.

Tauranga: Halo Brewing in Mount Maunganui has outdoor seating and a dog-friendly attitude. The Mount's beachside location means plenty of dog owners stop by after beach walks.

Brewery Etiquette: Being a Responsible Dog-Owner Patron

  • Keep your dog on a lead at all times in beer gardens and outdoor areas, even if the space is large.
  • Choose off-peak times. Weekday afternoons are quietest. Friday nights and Saturday brunch are peak dog-friendly hours — expect crowds.
  • Know your dog's limits. If your dog is anxious around crowds, noise, or other dogs, the pub is not the right outing.
  • Bring your own water bowl. Many venues provide bowls but it is courteous to bring your own collapsible bowl.
  • Do not feed your dog from the table. Pub food is often salty, greasy, and seasoned — all bad for dogs.
  • Clean up immediately. If your dog has an accident, inform staff and help clean up. It keeps venues dog-friendly for everyone.
  • Respect other patrons. Not everyone loves dogs. Keep your dog close and under control.
  • Check before you go. Policies change. Events, private functions, and kitchen inspections can affect dog access without notice.

Seasonal Considerations for NZ Pub Visits with Dogs

New Zealand's outdoor beer garden culture is glorious from October through April, but winter visits require more planning. Many breweries and pubs cover their outdoor areas with retractable roofs and patio heaters during colder months, but these spaces are smaller and fill up faster. In summer, beachside breweries are the go-to — combine a morning dog walk with an afternoon pint. In winter, look for breweries with covered courtyards and fire pits.

Summer also brings council-enforced dog restrictions on nearby beaches. Before planning a beach walk followed by a brewery visit, check your local council's seasonal dog access rules. Many popular beaches prohibit dogs during peak summer hours (typically 10am–7pm from December to February), which can throw a wrench in your walk-and-pint plans.
