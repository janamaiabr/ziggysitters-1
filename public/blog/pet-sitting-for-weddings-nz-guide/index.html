Pet Sitting for Weddings in New Zealand: A Practical Planning Guide

By ZiggySitters · 21 August 2026 · 9 min read

Your dog may be part of the family, but taking them to a wedding is not always as simple as bringing one more guest. Venues can have strict animal policies, long ceremony and reception hours are tiring, and well-meaning relatives may not be able to provide consistent supervision. A professional pet sitter lets you decide deliberately whether your pet attends part of the day or enjoys a calm routine elsewhere.

This guide explains how to plan wedding pet sitting in New Zealand, from the first venue conversation to the final handover. It applies whether your wedding is in Auckland, Wellington, Christchurch, Queenstown, a rural property or a beachside venue.

Start with the venue, not the sitter

Before booking care, ask the venue what is permitted. “Pet-friendly” can mean anything from a dog allowed in the grounds to a dog allowed only outside, on lead, and away from food preparation areas. Ask specifically about the ceremony, reception, accommodation, wet-weather plan and any noise or safety restrictions. A celebrant, photographer or venue coordinator may also have rules that are not obvious from the venue website.

If your pet will attend photos, appoint one adult as their dedicated handler. That person should not be the couple, a member of the wedding party with a schedule, or someone who will be drinking. The handler needs to carry waste bags, water, treats, leads and a towel, and should have authority to take the pet home when the animal has had enough.

Choose the right care arrangement

Most couples choose one of four options:

  • Home visits: A sitter feeds, toilets and exercises your pet at home. This suits cats, senior pets and animals that become overwhelmed by crowds.
  • House sitting: The sitter stays in your home, preserving the normal routine and providing company through a long wedding day or honeymoon.
  • Wedding-day attendance: A sitter brings your dog to agreed parts of the day and returns them home afterwards. This is the safest way to include a pet without making guests responsible.
  • Boarding at the sitter’s home: Useful when your accommodation is unsuitable or the wedding is several hours away. Confirm how many animals are present and how introductions are managed.

For many dogs, a short appearance followed by home care is the best compromise. They get the memorable photographs without enduring a six-hour reception, loud music and unfamiliar people.

Book early for peak dates

Wedding dates often overlap with Christmas, summer holidays, long weekends and school holidays. Those periods are also busy for pet sitters and accommodation. Contact sitters as soon as the date and venue are confirmed, ideally with a backup plan if the wedding runs late. Share the suburb or rural location, number and type of pets, required visits, transport expectations and whether anyone will be staying at the property.

Planning tip: Ask for a meet-and-greet before the wedding. Your pet should recognise the sitter, and the sitter should see gates, alarms, medication, food storage and the route to the nearest suitable vet.

Prepare a wedding-specific care brief

A normal feeding note is not enough when schedules change. Write down the exact handover time, ceremony start, photo window, expected return time and a person authorised to make decisions. Include:

  • feeding quantities, allergies and forbidden treats;
  • lead, harness, crate and escape-prevention instructions;
  • toilet routine, exercise limits and anxiety triggers;
  • medication doses and what to do if a dose is missed;
  • your regular vet, after-hours contact and microchip details;
  • the venue address, accommodation address and parking instructions;
  • permission for the sitter to seek veterinary care if you cannot be reached.

Leave supplies together in a labelled container. If your pet is appearing in photos, add a brush, towel, water bowl, stain remover, spare lead and a familiar blanket. Do not introduce a new food, outfit or grooming product immediately before the wedding.

Make participation low-stress

Dogs that attend should arrive after a normal meal and a quiet walk, not after being rushed from a car to a crowd. Keep them on a secure lead unless the venue has a genuinely safe enclosed area. Avoid letting guests feed them, and give them a retreat away from speakers, children and dancing. A dog that hides, pants continuously, trembles, lip-licks, growls or repeatedly tries to leave is communicating that the event is too much.

Cats rarely benefit from attending a wedding. Transport, unfamiliar smells and open doors create unnecessary risk. Home-based cat sitting is usually kinder, with a sitter checking food, water, litter, medication and enrichment at agreed intervals.

Transport across New Zealand

For weddings outside your home town, confirm who drives the pet and when. Use a properly secured crate or vehicle harness, never the front passenger seat. Plan toilet breaks and never leave an animal in a parked car, even on a mild day. New Zealand weather can change quickly: heat, cold, rain and wind all affect travel and outdoor ceremonies. For a South Island or rural venue, check mobile coverage and identify the nearest open veterinary clinic before the day.

Questions to ask a wedding pet sitter

  • Have you handled pets around crowds, children or loud music?
  • Will you be responsible for the pet for the whole agreed period?
  • How will you send updates without interrupting the wedding?
  • What happens if the ceremony or reception runs late?
  • Are transport, overnight care and extra visits included in the quote?
  • What is your emergency plan and cancellation policy?

Choose clear communication and a realistic plan over a promise that your pet can be everywhere. A sitter should be comfortable saying that a particular animal needs a quiet home day.

The handover checklist

On the day, allow enough time for a calm transfer. Confirm food, medication, keys, alarms, lead and emergency contacts verbally as well as in writing. Take a quick photo of the pet and supplies so everyone agrees what has been handed over. Ask for one update after the first visit and another when the pet is settled. At pickup, check that the animal has eaten, toileted and returned to their normal behaviour.

Your wedding should be joyful, not a day spent wondering whether the back gate is latched. With an early booking, a written care brief and a sitter who understands the animal’s limits, your pet can be safely included—or peacefully cared for at home.
