Pet-Friendly Home Design and Renovation in New Zealand

2026-07-31 · 10 min read · ZiggySitters Team

New Zealanders love their pets. With over 4.6 million companion animals across the country, our homes need to work just as well for our four-legged family members as they do for us. Whether you are building from scratch, planning a renovation, or simply making smart upgrades, designing a pet-friendly home is one of the most practical investments you can make. It reduces damage, keeps your animals safer, and creates a living space where everyone thrives.

This guide covers the key decisions Kiwi pet owners should consider when designing or renovating a home, from flooring and fencing to outdoor spaces and council regulations.

Flooring That Survives Paws, Claws, and Spills

Flooring is the single most impactful choice in a pet-friendly home. Dogs claws and cats claws behave differently on surfaces, and accidents happen. The right flooring can mean the difference between a home that looks great after ten years and one that needs replacing after two.

Best Flooring Options for Pet Owners

  • Luxury vinyl plank (LVP) — The top pick for most pet owners. LVP is waterproof, scratch-resistant, easy to clean, and affordable. Modern LVP mimics timber convincingly. Brands available in NZ such as Karndean and Polyflor offer residential warranties that cover pet damage in some cases.
  • Porcelain tile — Virtually indestructible. Resistant to scratches, moisture, and accidents. Excellent for wet areas like laundries and entryways where muddy paws congregate. Downsides: cold underfoot (consider underfloor heating) and hard on senior pet joints.
  • Polished concrete — Popular in NZ modern builds. Durable, easy to clean, and a good thermal mass for underfloor heating. Seal it properly and it handles spills well. Can be slippery for older dogs.
  • Engineered hardwood — A compromise if you love the look of timber. More resistant to moisture than solid wood, but still vulnerable to deep scratches from large dogs. Choose harder timber species like oak or jarrah.

Flooring to Avoid

  • Solid hardwood — Scratches easily, swells with moisture, and refinishing is expensive.
  • Carpet in high-traffic areas — Holds odours, stains permanently, and traps pet hair. Reserve carpet for bedrooms only, and choose low-pile, stain-treated options.
  • Laminate — The click-together stuff swells at the seams when wet. One accident and the joins are ruined.

Fencing and Boundaries: Keeping Pets Safe and Legal

New Zealand has specific legal requirements around dog fencing that vary by territorial authority. Under the Dog Control Act 1996, dog owners must ensure their dog does not wander at large. Most councils require fencing that prevents dogs from escaping, and some breeds classified as menacing must meet higher fencing standards.

Key Fencing Considerations

  • Height — Most NZ councils require at least 1.8 metre fencing for dogs. For breeds that jump (Greyhounds, Kelpies, Huskies), consider 2 metre fencing or a lean-in extension.
  • Material — Timber paling fences are the NZ standard. Ensure palings are close enough that small dogs cannot squeeze through. Coloursteel is another popular option that is durable and low-maintenance.
  • Digging barriers — Bury fencing mesh at least 30 cm underground or lay concrete along the fence line to prevent escape artists from digging out.
  • Gates — Self-closing, self-latching gates are essential. Place latches high enough that children cannot open them, and consider double-gate systems for driveways.
  • Cat-proofing — Cat enclosures (catios) are increasingly popular in NZ, especially in areas near native bird populations. Some councils encourage or require cats to be contained, particularly near conservation zones.

Outdoor Spaces Designed for Pets

A well-designed outdoor area gives your pet exercise, stimulation, and a toilet area while keeping your garden intact. In NZ, where many homes have generous sections, outdoor design for pets is especially relevant.

The Dog-Friendly Garden

  • Dedicated digging area — If your dog loves to dig, create a sanctioned sandpit. Bury toys and treats in it to redirect the behaviour away from your garden beds.
  • Durable plant choices — Avoid fragile ground covers near dog runs. Native grasses like carex and poa are resilient. Avoid plants toxic to dogs: karaka, oleander, rhododendron, lilies, and cycads are common NZ hazards.
  • Shade and shelter — NZ UV levels are extreme. Provide permanent shade structures, trees, or a well-ventilated kennel. Ensure outdoor water bowls are always in shade.
  • Artificial turf or gravel paths — High-traffic areas along fence lines (where dogs pace) and around doors will destroy grass. Lay paving, gravel, or artificial turf in these zones.
  • Pet-safe water features — If you want a water feature, ensure it has gently sloping sides so animals can exit. Steep-sided ponds are drowning hazards for small dogs and cats.

The Cat Enclosure (Catio)

Catios are outdoor enclosures that let cats enjoy fresh air and sunshine without roaming. In New Zealand, cat containment is increasingly important to protect native wildlife. Wellington, for example, has areas near Zealandia where cat containment is strongly encouraged.

A good catio includes multiple levels for climbing, weather-proof shelter, secure mesh (stainless steel or heavy-duty aviary mesh), and direct access from the house via a cat flap or window tunnel. Commercial catio builders operate in Auckland, Wellington, and Christchurch, and DIY plans are readily available online.

Indoor Design Features for Pet-Friendly Living

Mudroom and Entry Zones

New Zealand homes benefit enormously from a dedicated mudroom or entry zone. This is where muddy paws get wiped, wet coats get hung, and leads get stored. Include a bench for sitting (useful for putting on shoes and wrangling dogs), hooks for leads and collars, a washable floor, and a built-in dog towel rack. If space allows, add a dog wash station with a hand-held shower head at pet height.

Kitchen Design

Pets spend most of their indoor time in the kitchen. Design considerations include: wipe-clean surfaces (stone benchtops resist scratches), a dedicated feeding station with built-in bowls that does not take up floor space, pull-out drawers for pet food storage, and a closed bin for pet waste. Keep toxic foods stored in upper cabinets, not on low shelves where determined dogs can reach them.

Laundry as a Pet Zone

The laundry is often the de facto pet room in NZ homes. Make it work better with a walk-in shower or large trough for dog washing, floor drainage, durable wall cladding (tile or vinyl), and pet door access to the outdoors. This keeps muddy, wet dogs out of your main living areas.

Window Coverings

Cats will destroy sheer curtains. Dogs will push through vertical blinds. Practical choices include wooden or aluminium venetian blinds (durable, wipeable), roller blinds (replace slats are cheap), or washable curtains in hard-wearing fabrics like canvas. For cats who love window perches, install a wide windowsill or cat shelf that gives them a viewing spot without them clawing the curtains.

Safety Features Every Pet-Friendly Home Needs

  • Secure rubbish and recycling — Use pedal bins with locking lids or keep bins in a closed cupboard. Dogs will eat anything.
  • Cabinet latches — Child-proof latches on lower kitchen and bathroom cabinets prevent pets from accessing cleaning products and medications.
  • Fireplace screens — Open fireplaces are common in NZ homes. A sturdy mesh screen prevents curious pets from getting too close to embers.
  • Cord management — Cats love chewing cords. Use cord channels, cable trays, or furniture placement to keep electrical cords and blind cords out of reach.
  • Balcony safety — Apartment dwellers: ensure balcony railings have no gaps wider than 10 cm. Consider clear acrylic panels or vertical bars to prevent small pets from squeezing through or falling.
  • Non-toxic building materials — When renovating, choose paints, sealants, and adhesives with low VOCs. Let areas fully cure and ventilate before allowing pets back in.

NZ Building Code and Council Considerations

When renovating or building with pets in mind, keep these regulatory points in mind:

  • Dog Control Act 1996 — Requires dog owners to keep their dogs under control at all times. Your fencing must be adequate to contain your dog.
  • Menacing breed requirements — Some councils impose higher fencing requirements for classified menacing breeds. Check with your local council.
  • Resource consent for fencing — Fences over 2 metres typically require resource consent. Boundary fences require neighbour agreement under the Fencing Act 1978.
  • Cat containment zones — Some territorial authorities near ecological sanctuaries have cat containment bylaws. Check before designing your outdoor space.
  • Building consent — Structural changes like new walls, plumbing for a dog wash station, or electrical work require building consent. Cosmetic changes generally do not.

Budget-Friendly Pet-Friendly Upgrades

Not every improvement requires a full renovation. Here are high-impact, low-cost changes:

  1. Non-slip mats on polished floors — $30 to $80 per mat. Immediate safety improvement for senior dogs.
  2. Baby gates at stairs — $50 to $120. Restrict pet access to dangerous areas.
  3. Cat shelves and wall-mounted perches — $20 to $60 each. Vertical space reduces inter-cat conflict.
  4. Draft stoppers on doors — $15 to $40. Keeps warmth in and small pets from escaping under doors.
  5. Replace low cupboard knobs with child-proof latches — $5 to $15 each. Prevents pets from opening cabinets.
  6. Raised feeding station — $40 to $100. Better digestion for larger dogs and keeps the floor clean.
  7. Outdoor tap with hose connection near the door — $50 to $150. Muddy paws get washed before they enter.

Renovating with Pets at Home: Minimising Stress

Living through a renovation with pets is challenging. Construction noise, dust, unfamiliar people, and disrupted routines can cause significant anxiety. Practical steps include designating a safe room away from construction, maintaining feeding and walking schedules as strictly as possible, introducing the pet to tradespeople before work begins, and covering or sealing off dusty areas.

For major renovations, consider temporary relocation. Boarding facilities, pet-friendly short-term rentals, or a ZiggySitters host can keep your pet comfortable while work is underway. This is often less stressful for both you and your animal than trying to manage around construction.

Conclusion

Designing a pet-friendly home in New Zealand is not about sacrificing style or comfort for your animals. It is about making smart choices that work for the whole household. Durable flooring, secure fencing, practical outdoor spaces, and thoughtful indoor design mean your home looks great and functions well for every member of the family, whether they walk on two legs or four.

Whether you are building new, renovating, or making incremental improvements, every pet-friendly choice adds value to your home and quality to your pet is life. Start with the high-impact, low-cost upgrades, and work your way toward the bigger projects. Your pets will thank you for it.
