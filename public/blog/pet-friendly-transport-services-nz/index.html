Pet-Friendly Transport & Pet Moving Services in New Zealand

Published 5 August 2026 · 8 min read

Whether you're moving house across town, relocating from the North Island to the South Island, or simply need a ride to the vet, getting your pet from A to B in New Zealand requires planning. Not every taxi will accept a Golden Retriever in the back seat, and inter-island pet transport involves more than just buying a ferry ticket. This guide covers everything from local pet taxi services to full relocation companies, so you can transport your furry, feathered, or scaly family member safely and stress-free.

Why Pet-Specific Transport Matters

Standard transport options in New Zealand — from rideshares to public buses — generally don't accommodate pets. Even when they do, the experience can be stressful and unsafe for animals unfamiliar with travel. Pet-specific transport services provide:

  • Climate-controlled vehicles — essential during Auckland summers and Central Otago winters
  • Secure carriers and restraints — preventing injury during sudden stops
  • Trained handlers — who understand animal behaviour and stress signals
  • Vet coordination — for animals with medical needs or post-surgery transport
  • Insurance coverage — protecting your pet during transit

For pet sitters in New Zealand, knowing the local transport landscape is invaluable. Your clients may need help arranging transport for vet appointments, airport pickups, or emergency relocations — and being able to recommend reliable services sets you apart.

Local Pet Taxi Services

What Is a Pet Taxi?

Pet taxis are dedicated transport services designed specifically for animals. Unlike regular taxis or rideshares, pet taxis are equipped with proper carriers, harnesses, and climate control. Drivers are typically experienced with animals and can handle nervous, elderly, or post-surgical pets.

When to Use a Pet Taxi

  • Vet and specialist appointments when you can't drive
  • Grooming drop-offs and pick-ups
  • Daycare or boarding transfers
  • Airport transfers for pets travelling domestically
  • Emergency vet visits (many services offer after-hours transport)
  • Moving newly adopted pets to their forever home

Typical Costs

Pet taxi pricing in New Zealand varies by region and distance. As a general guide:

  • Short local trips (under 10 km): $25–$45
  • Medium distance (10–30 km): $45–$80
  • Long distance or airport runs: $80–$200+
  • After-hours or emergency: 1.5×–2× standard rate

Prices are typically higher in Auckland and Wellington than in smaller centres like Hamilton or Dunedin. Many services offer discounts for regular bookings — useful if your pet needs ongoing vet visits.

Major NZ Pet Taxi Providers

While availability changes, here are types of services commonly found across New Zealand's main centres:

  • Auckland: Multiple dedicated pet taxi services operate across the Auckland region, including North Shore, West Auckland, and South Auckland. Many offer airport transfers to and from Auckland Airport.
  • Wellington: Wellington-based services cover the wider region including the Hutt Valley and Porirua. The compact geography means shorter trip times and lower costs than Auckland.
  • Christchurch: Christchurch pet transport services cover the Canterbury region, with some offering runs to Banks Peninsula and Akaroa for holiday boarding.
  • Other centres: Hamilton, Tauranga, Palmerston North, and Dunedin all have at least one dedicated pet taxi or animal transport service. Rural areas may require advance booking for longer-distance pickup.

Inter-Island and Long-Distance Pet Transport

Moving Pets Between Islands

Relocating a pet between the North and South Islands is one of the most common long-distance transport needs in New Zealand. Options include:

  • Cook Strait Ferry (Interislander / Bluebridge): Pets can travel in your vehicle or in designated kennel areas. Booking in advance is essential, especially during summer and holidays. Pets stay in the car deck or supervised kennel area — you can visit them during the crossing but they cannot stay with you in passenger areas.
  • Air freight: Air New Zealand and other carriers offer domestic pet freight services between major airports. This is faster (under 2 hours flight time) but more expensive, typically $150–$400 depending on crate size and route.
  • Specialist pet relocation companies: These handle the entire process — from pickup at your door to delivery at your new home, including ferry or flight booking, crate hire, and customs documentation if needed.

Long-Distance Road Transport

Several companies specialise in road transport for pets across the North or South Island. These services typically use purpose-built vehicles with individual compartments, climate control, and regular rest stops. Key considerations:

  • Book at least 1–2 weeks in advance during peak periods
  • Ensure your pet's vaccinations are current (most services require proof)
  • Provide familiar bedding or a worn t-shirt for comfort
  • Notify the transport company of any medical conditions or behavioural issues
  • Avoid feeding for 4 hours before travel to reduce motion sickness

Air Travel with Pets in NZ

Domestic Flights

Air New Zealand is the primary domestic carrier accepting pets. Key points:

  • Pets travel as checked baggage in the cargo hold (climate-controlled and pressurised)
  • Small animals may be accepted in the cabin on some regional flights — check current policy
  • You must use an airline-approved travel crate (IATA compliant)
  • Book early — pet spots are limited per flight, typically 2–4 animals
  • Cost varies by crate size and route: $75–$300 domestic
  • Brachycephalic breeds (French Bulldogs, Pugs, Bulldogs) face additional restrictions and may not fly in warm weather

International Pet Transport

New Zealand has strict biosecurity requirements for importing pets. If you're moving to NZ with a pet or exporting one, specialist pet relocation services handle the complex paperwork, vet certifications, quarantine arrangements, and flight bookings. This is not a DIY process — MPI (Ministry for Primary Industries) requirements are detailed and strictly enforced.

Pet Transport for Pet Sitters

Services You Can Offer

As a pet sitter, transport-related services can differentiate your offering:

  • Vet run service: Pick up, transport to vet, and return. Charge $30–$60 on top of the vet bill for your time and transport.
  • Airport pet pickup: Collect arriving pets from domestic flights and deliver to their home or your sitting location.
  • Grooming taxi: Regular transport to and from grooming appointments — great for ongoing clients.
  • Emergency transport: After-hours vet runs are high value. Having a reliable vehicle and pet restraint system is essential.

Insurance and Legal Requirements

If you're transporting other people's pets professionally:

  • Your personal car insurance may not cover business use — notify your insurer or get a commercial policy
  • Consider pet transport insurance that covers the animal in your care
  • The Animal Welfare Act 2015 applies — you must ensure animals are transported safely with adequate ventilation, temperature control, and rest stops
  • Secure pets properly: harness and seatbelt for dogs, carriers for cats and small animals

Choosing the Right Transport Service

Questions to Ask Any Pet Transport Provider

  • Are you registered with NZTA as a commercial transport operator?
  • What insurance do you carry — for both vehicle and animal?
  • Can I see your vehicle setup (photos or in person)?
  • What happens if my pet needs emergency vet care during transport?
  • Do you provide updates during the journey (photos, GPS tracking)?
  • What are your cancellation and refund policies?
  • Can you handle special needs — senior pets, post-surgery, nervous animals?

Red Flags

  • No visible insurance or registration
  • Unwilling to let you see the vehicle
  • Multiple pets crammed into a single unventilated space
  • No clear emergency protocol
  • Significantly cheaper than other providers (corners are being cut somewhere)

Preparing Your Pet for Transport

Before the Trip

  • Vet check: Get a health certificate within 10 days of long-distance transport, especially for air travel
  • Crate training: Start crate training 2–4 weeks before any long trip. The crate should be a safe, positive space — not a punishment box
  • Short practice runs: Take your pet on progressively longer car trips to build tolerance
  • Update microchip and ID: Ensure your contact details are current on the NZ Companion Animal Register
  • Pack a travel kit: Food, water, medication, familiar blanket, leash, waste bags, and a photo of your pet

On the Day

  • Feed a light meal 4+ hours before departure (empty stomachs reduce motion sickness)
  • Exercise your dog before the trip to help them settle
  • Attach a tag with your phone number to the crate or carrier
  • Inform the transport driver of any quirks — "He barks at motorbikes" or "She gets car sick after 30 minutes"

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I take my pet on public transport in NZ?

In most cases, no. Auckland Transport, Metlink Wellington, and Environment Canterbury buses do not allow pets (except certified service dogs). Some ferry services make exceptions — check with the operator before travelling.

Can I use Uber or a regular taxi for my pet?

Some Uber drivers accept pets, but it's at the driver's discretion and you must select the "Pet Friendly" option where available. Traditional taxi companies vary — always call ahead to confirm. Pet taxis are the reliable option.

Do I need a special crate for air travel?

Yes. Airlines require IATA-compliant travel crates that meet size, ventilation, and construction standards. Most pet transport companies can hire these to you, or you can purchase them from pet stores or online.
