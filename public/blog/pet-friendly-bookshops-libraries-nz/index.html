Pet-Friendly Bookshops & Libraries in New Zealand

Published 30 July 2026 · ZiggySitters NZ

New Zealanders love two things deeply: their pets and a good book. So it makes perfect sense to combine the two. Across the country, a growing number of independent bookshops and public libraries welcome well-behaved dogs — and occasionally even cats — through their doors. Whether you are looking for a rainy-day outing in Auckland, a weekend browse in Wellington, or a quiet afternoon in Christchurch, this guide covers the best pet-friendly literary spots in New Zealand and how to make the most of them.

Why Take Your Pet to a Bookshop or Library?

Beyond the sheer novelty, there are solid practical reasons to bring your dog or cat along on a bookshop visit:

  • Socialisation practice. Calm, indoor environments expose your pet to new people, smells, and sounds without the overwhelming stimulation of a dog park.
  • Bonding time. Shared activities strengthen the human-animal bond, and a relaxed browse is quality time together.
  • Mental enrichment. New environments provide sensory enrichment that reduces boredom at home.
  • Weather-proof outings. When rain closes in — as it often does across NZ — an indoor bookshop visit beats another walk in the drizzle.
  • Community connection. Pet-friendly businesses are often community hubs where you meet like-minded owners.

Auckland: Pet-Friendly Bookshops

Auckland has a thriving independent bookshop scene, and several venues are known for their canine-friendly policies:

Shakespeare Books & Antiques (Ponsonby)

This Ponsonby institution has been welcoming dogs alongside their owners for years. The creaky wooden floors and floor-to-ceiling shelves create a cosy atmosphere where well-behaved dogs on lead are appreciated by staff and customers alike. Small dogs are especially popular here — the tight aisles suit compact companions better than large, excitable breeds.

Time Out Bookroom (Remuera)

Tucked away in Remuera, Time Out is a neighbourhood favourite that often has a resident dog lounging near the counter. Visiting dogs on lead are welcome, and the staff are happy to offer water bowls. It is the kind of place where your dog can settle under a table while you browse the curated selection.

Women's Bookshop (Ponsonby)

Another Ponsonby gem, the Women's Bookshop has a long-standing dog-friendly reputation. The spacious layout gives dogs room to settle, and the welcoming staff make both two-legged and four-legged visitors feel at home.

Little Unity (City Centre)

The smaller sibling of the iconic Unity Books, Little Unity in the city centre occasionally welcomes quiet dogs. Because the space is compact, it suits calm, well-trained dogs best. Call ahead if you are unsure.

Wellington: The Coolest Little Pet-Friendly Book Capital

Wellington is arguably NZ's bookshop capital, and its pet-friendly culture is strong:

Unity Books (Willis Street)

Wellington's iconic Unity Books on Willis Street has been a gathering place for readers and their dogs for decades. The generous layout and relaxed atmosphere make it one of the most dog-friendly bookshops in the country. You will often see dogs tucked under tables or greeting customers near the entrance.

Zeal Books (Cuba Street)

This Cuba Street second-hand bookshop is a treasure trove with an easy-going vibe. Dogs on lead are welcome, and the staff are genuine animal lovers. The slightly chaotic charm of Zeal Books means a calm dog will blend right in.

Featherston Bookshop (Featherston)

A short drive from Wellington in the Wairarapa, the Featherston Bookshop is worth the trip. This beautifully curated independent shop welcomes dogs and even has treats behind the counter. Combined with a walk on the nearby Remutaka Rail Trail, it makes a perfect day out.

Wellington Central Library

While most public libraries in NZ do not allow pets inside, Wellington Central Library has hosted pet-friendly reading events in the past. The adjacent waterfront and civic square are great for a walk before or after your library visit. Always check current policies before bringing a pet.

Christchurch & Canterbury

Paper Plus (Various Locations)

Several Paper Plus stores in Christchurch have pet-friendly managers who welcome quiet dogs. The larger-format stores with wide aisles are particularly suitable. It varies by location, so a quick phone call ahead saves disappointment.

Scorpio Books (Cashel Street)

In the Re:Start mall area, Scorpio Books has a reputation for being accommodating to well-behaved dogs. The spacious, light-filled store gives dogs plenty of room to settle while you explore their extensive New Zealand section.

The Christchurch City Library Network

Christchurch's modern library network, including Tūranga — the stunning central library — does not generally allow pets inside. However, the outdoor spaces surrounding Tūranga and the Avon River precinct are excellent for a walk with your dog before you head to a nearby pet-friendly cafe.

Dunedin & the South

University Book Shop (Otago)

Dunedin's University Book Shop has a long tradition of welcoming dogs, reflecting the city's relaxed, student-friendly culture. The knowledgeable staff are often dog owners themselves and happy to see well-mannered canine visitors.

Hard to Find Books (Opoho)

This beloved second-hand bookshop in the suburb of Opoho is the kind of rambling, multi-room treasure where a quiet dog on lead feels right at home. Call ahead to confirm, as policies can vary with staff on duty.

Regional Gems Worth a Road Trip

  • Marilyn's Bookshop (Gisborne): A charming independent with a warm welcome for dogs and their owners.
  • Volume Bookshop (Nelson): Nelson's premier indie bookshop is known for its community focus and dog-friendly attitude.
  • Wardini Books (Havelock North): A Hawke's Bay favourite with a carefully curated selection and a genuine welcome for leashed dogs.
  • Paper Plus (Cambridge): This Waikato town's Paper Plus has a loyal following of dog-owning customers who appreciate the welcoming policy.

Library Policies Across NZ: What You Need to Know

Public libraries in New Zealand generally do not allow pets inside the building. This is due to health regulations, allergy concerns, and the need to maintain a quiet environment. However, there are important exceptions and alternatives:

  • Pet-friendly events: Some libraries host pet-themed reading programmes, particularly during school holidays, where well-behaved dogs are invited for special sessions.
  • Assistance dogs: Certified guide dogs, hearing dogs, and service dogs are legally permitted in all public libraries under the Human Rights Act 1993.
  • Outdoor reading spaces: Many libraries have adjacent parks, courtyards, or waterfront areas that are perfect for reading with your dog nearby.
  • Little free libraries: These community book-swap boxes found in many NZ neighbourhoods are perfectly pet-friendly — your dog can wait beside you while you pick up your next read.
Ziggy Tip: Before visiting any bookshop with your pet, call ahead or check their social media. Pet policies can change, and some venues may restrict pets during busy periods or events.

How to Prepare Your Pet for a Bookshop Visit

A successful bookshop outing requires preparation, especially if it is your dog's first time indoors in a retail environment:

Before You Go

  • Practise settle behaviour. Your dog should be able to lie calmly at your feet for 15-30 minutes. Practice this at home and at pet-friendly cafes first.
  • Tire them out slightly. A short walk before the visit takes the edge off. Not exhausted — just not bouncing off the walls.
  • Bring essentials: a portable water bowl, waste bags, and a quiet chew or Kong to keep them occupied.
  • Groom beforehand. A clean, brushed dog is a more welcome visitor. This is especially important for bookshops with fabric furniture.
  • Check the forecast. On wet days, your dog will have muddy paws. Consider a quick paw wash or towel-dry before entering.

During the Visit

  • Keep your dog on a short lead. A 1-metre lead gives you control in tight aisles without restricting comfortable settling.
  • Stay aware of your surroundings. Not everyone is comfortable around dogs. Keep your dog close and be ready to step aside.
  • Avoid the food and cafe sections. Food areas in bookshops are typically off-limits for pets due to health regulations.
  • Watch for stress signals: lip licking, yawning, whale eye (showing the whites of eyes), panting when not hot, or a tucked tail. These mean it is time to leave.
  • Reward calm behaviour. Carry small, low-odour treats and reward your dog for settling quietly.

Can You Take a Cat to a Bookshop?

It is less common, but some bookshops have welcomed cats — both visiting and resident. The key differences from dogs:

  • Carrier required. Unless your cat is exceptionally well-trained on a harness, a secure carrier is non-negotiable. Bookshops are full of escape routes and hiding spots.
  • Harness training first. If you want your cat out of the carrier, invest in harness training at home first. Start with short sessions and gradually increase duration.
  • Call ahead. Fewer bookshops are prepared for cats than dogs. A phone call avoids awkward arrivals.
  • Consider temperament. Only calm, confident cats who enjoy new environments should visit. A frightened cat benefits nothing from the experience.
Important: Never bring a cat to a bookshop that has a resident cat. Territory disputes between cats in an enclosed space create a stressful experience for both animals and everyone in the shop.

Pet Etiquette for Bookshops: The Unwritten Rules

Bookshop visits are a privilege, not a right. Following these etiquette guidelines helps keep pet-friendly policies in place:

  1. Always ask before entering. Even if the shop has been pet-friendly before, policies change. A quick "Is it okay if my dog comes in?" goes a long way.
  2. Small dogs can be carried. If your small dog is nervous or the shop is crowded, picking them up keeps them calm and out from underfoot.
  3. Never let your dog chew books. This should be obvious, but puppies in particular may see shelves of chewable objects as a buffet. Watch them constantly.
  4. Clean up immediately. If an accident happens — and it can, even with well-trained dogs — inform staff immediately and offer to pay for any damage.
  5. Respect other customers. Keep your dog close, do not allow them to approach strangers without invitation, and be prepared to leave if your dog is unsettled.
  6. Leave no trace. No shedding, no mud, no wet paw prints. A quick brush and paw wipe before entering makes a huge difference.

Combining Bookshop Visits With Other Pet-Friendly Outings

Make a day of it by pairing your bookshop visit with other pet-friendly stops:

  • Auckland: Time Out Bookshop in Remuera + a walk through nearby Cornwall Park or the Remuera Village cafes
  • Wellington: Unity Books + waterfront walk + pet-friendly cafe on Cuba Street
  • Christchurch: Scorpio Books + Hagley Park walk + Riverside Market (outdoor areas)
  • Dunedin: University Book Shop + a walk through the Botanic Garden + a stop at a dog-friendly cafe on George Street
  • Nelson: Volume Bookshop + a walk along the Maitai River + Founders Park

Seasonal Considerations

New Zealand's changeable weather makes bookshop visits particularly appealing at certain times of year:

  • Winter (June-August): Cold, wet days make indoor outings ideal. Bookshop visits provide mental enrichment when outdoor walks are shorter.
  • Summer (December-February): On scorching days, bookshops offer air-conditioned relief. Visit mid-afternoon when pavements are too hot for paws.
  • Spring (September-November): Unpredictable showers make bookshops a reliable backup plan. Keep a list of pet-friendly shops on your phone.
  • Autumn (March-May): The best season for combining outdoor walks with indoor browsing — crisp air outside, warm and dry inside.

Final Checklist Before You Go

  • Call the bookshop to confirm their current pet policy
  • Pack a portable water bowl and a quiet chew toy
  • Bring waste bags and a small towel for muddy or wet paws
  • Ensure your dog's registration and microchip details are up to date
  • Practise settle behaviour at home before trying it in public
  • Choose a quiet time for your first visit — weekday mornings are ideal
  • Have an exit plan if your pet becomes stressed or overstimulated
